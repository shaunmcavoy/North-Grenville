Picture the first really cold night of the year. You're standing at the back door looking at the hot tub. Part of you wants to get in. Another part of you is wondering what this is going to do to the hydro bill.

I don't want you to have that second thought. A hot tub is supposed to be the easy part of your day, the place you go to let the week fall off your shoulders and sit with the people you love. If you're doing math in your head every time you lift the cover, the tub has stopped doing its job.

So here's the short answer. Keeping a hot tub affordable in an Ontario winter doesn't come from using it less. It comes from a well-insulated tub, a good cover that stays on, clean water and filters, and one heating habit that surprises a lot of people: set the temperature you like and leave it there.

Winter is when your tub matters most. The days are short around Kemptville, everyone drifts inside, and it's easy to go weeks without much fresh air or real conversation. I'd hate for worry about cost to be the thing that keeps you from it.

## Simple habits that keep the bill down

Here's what I tell every owner heading into winter.

- **Set it and forget it.** Pick the temperature you enjoy and leave it there all winter. It takes more hydro to let the water cool off and heat it back up than to hold it steady, and reheating a tub full of cold water takes a long time. Don't turn it down between soaks, and don't count on warming it up right before you get in.
- **Keep the cover on and latched.** Your cover holds in most of your heat. Put it back on every time you get out, even if you're only stepping inside for a minute. Keep it latched so wind can't lift the edge.
- **Check your cover's condition.** Over time a cover can soak up water and lose its seal, letting heat escape. If yours feels much heavier than it used to, or you can see steam leaking out, it may be time for a new one. Text me and I'll tell you honestly whether it is.
- **Brush off the snow.** Use a soft broom. Heavy wet snow can bend a cover out of shape, and a bent cover doesn't seal well.
- **Rinse your filters once a month.** Clean filters let the water move freely, so the pump and heater aren't straining. Follow your tub's manual for how to clean and when to replace them.
- **Keep the water clean.** Our water around North Grenville is hard, and scale on the heater makes it work harder to do the same job. A weekly mineral treatment like O-Care keeps scale and biofilm down with fewer chemicals. If you bought your tub from us, [register your O-Care with me here](https://l.ead.me/bgkT4n). It's delivered free to your door with a saving on every box, so you never run out on a snowy week, and you can pause or cancel any time.
- **Block the wind if you can.** A fence, a privacy screen or the side of the house between your tub and the prevailing wind helps the cover hold its heat on the coldest nights.
- **Plan water changes for a milder day.** When it's time to drain and refill, pick a day that isn't bitterly cold, have everything ready, and get the tub refilled and heating again the same day.

If you're still shopping, ask about insulation, the heater and the cover, not just the jets. Those three things decide what the tub costs you every winter for years. ## Spend the winter in it, not worrying about it

A hot tub should feel like a gift you give your family every night, not a bill you brace for. Get the basics right, set your temperature once, and keep the cover snug. Then the only thing left to think about on a cold January night is who's coming out with you.
