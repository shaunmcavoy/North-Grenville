Hot tub care in hard water: a North Grenville guide

Blaine McAvoy
Owner, North Grenville Hot Tubs

If you live in Kemptville or anywhere in North Grenville, there's a good chance your water is hard. That's fine for drinking, but it's tough on a hot tub. The good news is that hard water is easy to manage once you know what's going on, and it doesn't take a cupboard full of chemicals.

Here's what I tell every customer before their tub is delivered.

What hard water actually is

Hard water is water that carries a lot of dissolved minerals, mostly calcium and magnesium. You've probably seen the signs at home already: the white crust on a kettle, spots on glasses, or a ring around the shower head.

A hot tub takes that same water and heats it, day and night. Heat makes those minerals come out of the water and stick to whatever they touch. In a hot tub, that means the heater, the jets, the plumbing and the shell.

What it does to your tub

Mineral buildup is called scale, and it causes problems slowly, so most people don't notice until something goes wrong.

  • Scale on the heater. A heater with a coat of scale has to work harder to warm the water. That costs you on your hydro bill and wears the heater out sooner.
  • Jets that weaken or clog. Scale narrows the openings, so the massage you paid for slowly gets weaker.
  • A rough line around the shell. That chalky ring at the waterline is scale, and it's harder to clean the longer it sits.
  • Cloudy or dull water. Minerals can make the water look hazy even when it's sanitized.

Scale also gives biofilm a place to hide. Biofilm is the slimy layer that grows inside the pipes where you can't see it, fed by body oils, lotions and sweat. Biofilm protects bacteria from your sanitizer, which is why a lot of owners end up adding more and more chemicals just to keep the water looking right.

Why more chemicals isn't the answer

This is where a lot of hot tub owners get stuck. The water goes cloudy, so they add something. It smells, so they shock it. It foams, so they add a bottle of something else. Every fix adds another layer to the water, and none of it deals with the real problem underneath, which is buildup.

I think of it like eating. You can live on processed food and feel fine for a while, but it catches up with you. Eat clean and your body runs better and lasts longer. Your hot tub is the same. I call it putting your hot tub on a diet.

A simple hard-water routine

You don't need to be a chemist. Here's the routine I recommend for hard water around here.

  1. Fill through a hose pre-filter. A simple filter on the end of your garden hose catches a lot of minerals and metals before they ever get into the tub. It's one of the cheapest things you can do.
  2. Test the water once a week. Use test strips and keep your levels in the range the strip and your tub's manual recommend. Balanced water is much less likely to form scale.
  3. Use a weekly mineral treatment like O-Care. This is what I set my customers up with. If you bought your tub from us, register your O-Care with me here and it's delivered free to your door, so you never run out. It goes in once a week and works on scale and biofilm at the source, so your sanitizer can do its job with a lot less help. Less junk in, more years out.
  4. Rinse your filters every month. Hard water clogs filters faster. A good rinse with a hose keeps water moving and takes some strain off the pump. Plan to replace them every six to twelve months.
  5. Drain and refill on schedule. Even with good care, minerals build up over time. Follow your tub's manual for how often to change the water, and give the shell a wipe while it's empty.

That's it. It adds up to a few minutes a week.

Which tubs handle hard water best?

Any well-built tub handles hard water fine with the routine above. Where you'll notice a difference is in how much help the water needs.

Every Platinum tub I sell comes with ozone sanitation, which does a lot of the cleaning for you. If you want the water to take care of itself as much as possible, the Luxury line adds UV and Platinum Pure on top of that, plus four-stage filtration. For a lot of families, the Premium line is the sweet spot, and the routine above keeps the water soft with very little effort.

The bottom line

Hard water doesn't have to mean a hard time. Fill smart, test weekly, keep your filters clean, and treat the cause instead of piling on chemicals. Your water will feel softer, your heater and jets will last longer, and you'll spend your evenings in the tub instead of fussing with it.
