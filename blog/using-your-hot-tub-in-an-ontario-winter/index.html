Using your hot tub in an Ontario winter

Blaine McAvoy
Owner, North Grenville Hot Tubs

Picture a Tuesday night in January. It's dark by five, the driveway needs shovelling again, and everyone in the house has drifted off to a different screen. Then somebody says, "Tub?" A few minutes later you're sitting in warm water with snow on the railing, steam rising into the cold air, and the people you love most right beside you. The week feels a lot lighter.

People sometimes ask me if a hot tub is really a summer thing. It's the opposite. Our winters around Kemptville are long, and they're the months when most of us stop going outside and start hibernating in front of the TV. A hot tub gets you out the back door and into the fresh air, all season long.

So, can you use your hot tub all winter in Ontario? Yes, and you should. You don't need to drain it or shut it down when the snow comes. A well-built tub is made to run right through the cold, and with a few simple habits it stays easy, warm and worry-free until spring.

Winter is when it matters most

In more than fifteen years selling hot tubs, what I hear over and over from owners is that they use their tub more in winter than they ever expected. A lot of people find it's the best part of the day after shovelling, a long drive home or a cold game at the rink.

It's also a reset for the whole family. Short days can make a house feel small. Twenty minutes outside together, with no phones, changes the mood of an evening. I wrote more about that in the hot tub habit.

That's what I want for you. Not a tub you worry about all winter, but one you actually look forward to.

Built for our winters, kept simple

When I help someone pick a tub, winter is always part of the conversation. A tub that loses heat all winter isn't a good deal. That's why I sell Platinum Spas. The Deluxe line has Premium Shield insulation with a full wrap around the cabinet and an Aristech acrylic shell built for Ontario winters. The Premium line adds five types of insulation in the cabinet and recycles the waste heat from the pumps back into the water, which helps keep winter running costs down.

I also believe winter care should be simple. A lot of dealers make owning a tub sound complicated so you'll keep buying products. I'd rather show you a short routine you'll actually keep: a good cover, clean water and a bit of common sense.

And if something ever doesn't look right on a cold night, call or text me. I answer my own phone, and I'd much rather hear from you early than have you guess.

Your winter checklist

Here's what I tell every owner before the first real cold snap.

  • Keep it full and running. Leave the tub on and heating all winter. Moving water that's kept warm is what keeps the plumbing safe. If your power goes out for a long time in deep cold, or you're planning to be away, call me so we can talk it through.
  • Keep the cover on and latched. Put it back on every time you get out, even for a few minutes. The cover holds in most of your heat, and the latches keep it secure in the wind.
  • Brush off the snow. Use a soft broom, not a shovel, and never chip at ice with anything sharp. Heavy, wet snow left sitting on a cover puts a lot of weight on it.
  • Make the walk easy. Keep a clear, non-slippery path to the tub, slip-on boots by the door, and a towel and robe within reach.
  • Keep the water clean and simple. Test once a week and use a weekly mineral treatment like O-Care to keep scale and biofilm down in our hard water. If you bought your tub from us, register your O-Care with me here. It's delivered free to your door with a saving on every box, so you never run out on a snowy week, and you can pause or cancel any time. My hard water guide has the full routine.
  • Rinse your filters monthly. It takes a few minutes and keeps the water moving well all winter.
  • Plan your water changes. When it's time to drain and refill, pick a milder day, have everything ready before you start, and get the tub refilled and heating again the same day. Follow your tub's manual for the steps.

A few comfort tips for the soak itself:

  • Wear a toque. Your body stays warm in the water, and a hat keeps your head comfortable. Wet hair freezes fast, so keep it dry or covered.
  • Keep the temperature comfortable, not as hot as it goes, and drink some water. Get out slowly, since the cold air can make you feel lightheaded.
  • Kids need shorter soaks and cooler water, with an adult always there. If you have a health condition, are pregnant or take medication, check with your doctor about soaking.

If you'd like the tub ready the minute you get home, the Luxury line has Wi-Fi app control, so you can warm it up from your phone on the drive.

Make this the winter you use it

Winter in North Grenville is long, but it doesn't have to feel that way. A warm tub under a cold sky turns the darkest months into some of the best nights of the year, and with a few easy habits, the tub takes care of itself while you take care of each other. Lift the cover, step in and look up. That's what it's for.
